Motivation (The Why)

In modern Chinese society, the rapid pace of urbanization and digital transformation has created a growing emotional gap between generations. Younger family members are deeply immersed in digital culture, while grandparents often feel disconnected from the fast-evolving technological world. Traditional family gatherings, such as the Chinese New Year's Eve dinner, remain one of the few moments where all generations come together. However, even during these precious occasions, meaningful intergenerational interaction is often lacking — younger members may be absorbed in their phones while elders sit quietly [1].

Research shows that the mere presence of mobile devices degrades in-person social interaction quality [1], while intergenerational storytelling has been proven to strengthen family identity and emotional bonding [2]. Game-based approaches have also shown promise in fostering communication between generations in family settings [3]. Yet existing communication tools like WeChat lack dedicated features for cross-generational shared activities, elder-friendly interfaces, or collaborative experiences that leverage each generation's strengths.

Bridge of Time aims to transform these shared moments into playful, engaging experiences that celebrate cultural heritage while fostering genuine emotional connections across generations. By designing a mobile web application with inclusive, accessible interactions, we hope to create a "bridge" that connects the wisdom of the past with the energy of the present.

Interview Insights (5 Grandparents, aged 60+)

Q: How often do you see your grandchildren?
"Not very often. Their parents are busy with work, and we don't live close by. We usually only get together for dinner on weekends."
Q: Do you find it easy to chat on your phone?
"Not really. The text is too small, my eyesight is bad. There are too many functions, and I can't remember how to use them all."
Q: What's the biggest difficulty talking to grandchildren?
"No common topics! I don't understand the games, influencers, or internet slang they talk about, and they're not interested in my stories."
Q: What kind of tool would help you communicate better?
"I just want something simple! Big text, big buttons, no learning curve. Ideally one-tap video calls and voice messages."
Q: What do current chat apps lack?
"Intergenerational interaction features! They're all made for young people, with no content designed for grandparents and kids to use together."

Key Requirements

Based on our user research (surveys, interviews, and literature review), we identified 3 must-have requirements for Bridge of Time to be considered a "playful" intergenerational experience

1

Shared Collaborative Activities

The system must provide activities that require both generations to participate together — neither can complete the task alone. This creates positive interdependence [5] and gives both generations a reason to engage. Research shows that game-based interactions effectively foster intergenerational communication [3]. Our survey shows 63.2% want to share photos/stories together, and 36.8% want simple games to play together.

Survey: 63.2% want shared activities Interview: "I wish we could do something together"
2

Elder-Friendly Accessible Interface

The interface must use large touch targets, high-contrast visuals, simple navigation, and voice input as a primary interaction mode for elderly users [6]. 42.1% of respondents say ease-of-use is the most valued feature, and interview participants consistently described current apps as "too small" and "too complicated."

Survey: 42.1% prioritise ease of use Interview: "Big text, big buttons, open and use right away"
3

Reversed Knowledge Hierarchy

The system must include features where grandparents are the experts — sharing traditions, recipes, and cultural knowledge — reversing the typical tech-teaching dynamic [7][8]. This restores elders' sense of value and gives grandchildren genuine reasons to listen, not out of politeness but curiosity.

Literature: Reverse mentoring boosts elder self-efficacy [8] Interview: "Nobody wants to listen to my old stories"

Ideation & Design Alternatives

Exploring multiple approaches before converging on our final design

Crazy 8s Sketching

Our team conducted rapid ideation sessions using the Crazy 8s method, generating 8 quick UI concepts in 8 minutes. Key ideas that emerged: voice-first storytelling, collaborative cooking timers, cultural trivia battles, and shared photo timelines.

Alternative A

Video-Call Add-on

A WeChat mini-program that overlays interactive games on video calls between grandparents and grandchildren.

Pros: Leverages existing platform, low friction to adopt
Cons: Limited by WeChat API restrictions, no standalone identity, poor offline support
Rejected: too dependent on third-party platform constraints
Alternative B

AR Family Scrapbook

A tangible-first approach: physical photo album that triggers AR animations when scanned with a phone camera.

Pros: Physical artifact grandparents can hold, novel XR experience
Cons: Requires physical printing, high tech barrier for elders to initiate AR, limited scalability
Rejected: tech barrier contradicts elder-accessibility requirement
Chosen

Playful Activity Hub (Bridge of Time)

A standalone mobile web app with role-based UI that provides collaborative activities (stories, cooking, quiz, album) designed for same-room use during family gatherings.

Pros: No install needed (PWA), role-based complexity, interdependent tasks, works offline, elder-friendly UI
Cons: Requires both parties to have a phone, no real-time sync between devices (single-device prototype)
Chosen: best balance of accessibility, engagement, and technical feasibility

Prototypes & Iteration

Key design improvements made based on peer feedback and design review

Iteration 1

Elder Accessibility — Large Font Mode

Problem identified: Early feedback from peer reviewers acting as grandparent users highlighted difficulty reading small text and tapping small buttons on the prototype.

Before

  • Fixed 12-14px font sizes across all screens
  • Standard touch targets (36px)
  • No accessibility options available

After

  • Large Font Mode scales text by 30-50%
  • Touch targets enlarged to 52px+
  • Settings accessible from role selection screen (⚙️ gear icon on Grandparent card)
  • Context-based: uses React Context to propagate across all screens
Impact: Significantly improved accessibility for elderly users, directly addressing our Requirement #2 (Elder-Friendly Accessible Interface) and the survey finding that 42.1% prioritise ease of use.
Iteration 2

Playful Interaction — Quiz Celebration

Problem identified: The Traditions Quiz ended abruptly after the final question with no sense of achievement or positive reinforcement, reducing the "playful" experience.

Before

  • Quiz ended with a simple "Quiz Complete" text
  • No emotional reward or celebration
  • Score shown in plain text

After

  • Confetti animation plays on quiz completion
  • Animated score reveal with encouraging messages
  • Role-specific praise (acknowledging grandparent as "tradition expert")
  • Replay and share options for continued engagement
Impact: Reinforced positive feedback loops, making the shared quiz experience more joyful and memorable — strengthening engagement and the "play as social lubricant" design rationale [3][5].

Evaluation

Peer review and heuristic evaluation of the Bridge of Time prototype

Method

We conducted a peer evaluation with 3 classmates (acting as proxy users for both grandchild and grandparent personas). Each evaluator completed a set of tasks on the prototype and provided feedback on usability, accessibility, and engagement.

Task Success Rate Avg. Time Notes

Add a photo to Memory Album 3/3 (100%) ~12s Timeline layout intuitive

Heuristic Evaluation Summary (Nielsen's 10 Heuristics)

Strengths

  • Visibility of system status: Clear navigation, back buttons on every screen, active state indicators
  • Match with real world: Uses familiar metaphors (photo album, recipe steps, quiz cards)
  • User control: Back navigation always available, settings easily accessible
  • Aesthetic design: Clean, warm visual style with consistent colour palette

Areas for Improvement

  • Error prevention: No confirmation when leaving a story recording mid-way
  • Help & documentation: No onboarding tutorial for first-time users
  • Flexibility: Large Font Mode toggle location not immediately obvious to all users

Qualitative Feedback

Reflection

Critical reflection on social impact, ethical considerations, and AI usage

Social & Ethical Impact

Positive impact: Bridge of Time addresses a genuine social challenge — the intergenerational communication gap accelerated by technology. By designing collaborative activities where both generations contribute unique strengths, we promote mutual respect and preserve cultural heritage that might otherwise be lost.

Inclusivity: The Large Font Mode and role-based UI complexity demonstrate our commitment to inclusive design. We ensure that elderly users with lower digital literacy can participate meaningfully without feeling infantilised.

Privacy considerations: Family stories and photos are sensitive personal data. In a production system, we would implement end-to-end encryption and ensure data stays within the family unit. Our prototype currently stores no user data (stateless), which is an intentional privacy-by-design choice for the demonstration phase.

Cultural sensitivity: The app centres on Spring Festival traditions, which may not represent all Chinese families equally. Future work should expand to diverse cultural backgrounds and avoid reinforcing stereotypes about elderly people's capabilities.

AI & Vibe Coding Reflection

Tools used: Claude (Anthropic) was used as the primary AI coding assistant for developing the React prototype. All design decisions, user research, and design rationale are original team work.

How AI was used:

  • UI Component Generation: Prompts were used to scaffold React screen components (SplashScreen, HomeScreen, QuizScreen, etc.) based on our Figma wireframes and design specifications
  • Accessibility Implementation: AI helped implement the FontContext system and Large Font Mode propagation across all screens
  • Animation: The confetti celebration animation in QuizScreen was AI-generated based on our interaction design brief
  • Deployment: AI assisted with Vite configuration and GitHub Pages deployment setup

How we validated AI-generated code:

  • Manual testing of all user flows on multiple device sizes
  • Peer review against our design requirements document
  • Checking accessibility standards (touch target sizes, contrast ratios, font scaling)
  • Iterative refinement based on team discussion and peer feedback

Ethical considerations:

  • Accessibility: We verified that AI-generated components met WCAG guidelines for font sizes, contrast, and touch targets for elderly users
  • Bias: We reviewed AI-generated content for cultural assumptions and stereotypes about elderly users
  • Transparency: All AI tool usage is documented in our /ai-logs folder and referenced below
